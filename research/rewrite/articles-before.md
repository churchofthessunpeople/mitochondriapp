### Mitochondria as light- and field-driven electronics

Mitochondria form one "bus bar" network, so a local failure spreads body-wide; tissues sync through terahertz signals across 20–50 nm gaps. Mitochondria are, he says, photosynthesis run in reverse, which is why light therapy works by resonance. Iron cores in ferritin are "ferrons" (tiny magnetic compass-capacitors) that leak iron when a person is unpinned. Swelling and fragmentation are real-time responses to stress — a record of the environment, not the disease.

The fusion site, he adds, is wired to four genes that shaped modern humans: POMC (melanin), EDAR (sweat cooling), GCG (the gut's exhaust signal) and BCL11A (fetal hemoglobin).

Spirals order water — "three turns sense, seven turns resonate" — and the heart's helical chambers, he says, spin blood this way to manage deuterium.

In spin terms, ortho-water is the "grease" of 78 water and para-water the "superconductor" of 160; CO₂ holding pH near 6.8–7.1 tips water toward para.

### Oxygen's spin and the paramagnetic gate
- **id:** magnetic-excursion-oxygen
- **pillar:** magnetism
- **summary:** In his model Earth's field keeps oxygen in its safe triplet state at the end of the electron chain, and a weakening field flips it to reactive singlet oxygen.

Ordinary oxygen is triplet: its two outer electrons spin in parallel, making it paramagnetic (weakly drawn to magnets) and unable to react directly with ordinary molecules — which, Kruse says, is what makes it a safe final electron acceptor. At cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) the copper–iron center is a "paramagnetic gate" (a field-backed spin-matching checkpoint): backed by the geodynamo, spin-aligned electrons tunnel in smoothly, oxygen becomes deuterium-depleted water and the membrane holds about −70 mV. Since magnetism controls spin, a weakening core field now pushes oxygen toward singlet in many zip codes, "liquidating" tissue. nnEMF, isolated blue light or deuterium do the same locally: oxygen flips, attacks cardiolipin and mitochondrial DNA, voltage falls toward −15 mV and clean-water production stops. Brief superoxide pulses are healthy signals, which he says diabetics have exhausted. Citing what he calls an ESA report that the inner core reversed its rotation, he sees field chaos driving cells toward fermentation.

The triplet–singlet choice is also his "master switch" for the cell's light: triplet oxygen releases energy quietly, while singlet oxygen's lipid damage throws out disordered photon bursts that drown the coherent UV signals guiding cell division. Melanin, DHA and cholesterol help a strong dipole hold the spins; moving to a different field flips oxygen at once.

### Marcus inversion and the Warburg shift
- **id:** marcus-theory-inverted-region
- **pillar:** light
- **summary:** Kruse's core failure state: electron transfer stalls in Marcus's "inverted region," and cells retreat to fermentation as a protective emergency mode.
- **protocols:** none

Marcus's theory describes how fast electrons jump between molecules; past a certain driving force the rate falls again — the "inverted region." Kruse makes this his central failure state. Field loss, nnEMF, blue light or calcium overload push the chain past the point where more voltage helps, and a weaker field widens the electron's orbit beyond the 14 Å tunneling limit, stopping the chain physically — a "cyanide-like" lock without the poison.

He increasingly frames it as protection: a built-in circuit breaker ("topological insurance") that clamps electron flow before a thermal meltdown. A place or season can hold someone there — a "Marcus inversion well" — which fat mass and seasonal timing soften.

Warburg fermentation is, in his model, an ancient survival mode: as the field falls, cells lower their voltage and retreat to glycolysis to shield DNA. When both light-run clean-ups fail (UV-driven cell death, near-infrared recycling), a damaged cell can neither repair nor die. Field loss also triggers a "biological SCRAM" (emergency shutdown): mitochondrial DNA leaks, the cGAS-STING alarm fires and Naviaux's cell danger response switches on — his explanation for early cancer, faster aging and neurodegeneration. Building on Warburg, cancer cells fall from about −70 mV to −15/−30 mV and deuterium-rich water floods the matrix, so the Warburg question is one of spin, not chemistry.

### Lattice lock
- **id:** lattice-lock
- **pillar:** water
- **summary:** His name for the end state of water collapse: body water stiffens and loads with deuterium, the energy motor jams, and the cell can no longer make its own light water or use sunlight.

The cell leaks chaotic light ("radiant smoke").

**Where it shows.** In the brainstem, deuterium filtering fails at the fourth ventricle and heavy water backs up in his "sphenoid Venturi" (the drain point behind the nose), blocking spinal-fluid and vagal drainage. **How he reads it.** Heart-rate variability (HRV, beat-to-beat timing variation) is the readout: rigid, flat HRV is complete lock, and flexible deep-sleep HRV is his progress gauge. Warning signs run from chronic nasal congestion to fatigue and memory loss to low HRV; a lost sun-sneeze reflex and trouble tolerating carbonated water show depth. He pairs it with three release routines: the Volcano Protocol, hypnosis and sunrise shoreline breathing.
