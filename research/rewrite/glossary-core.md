## Glossary: Core model

| Term | Plain meaning (in his usage) |
|---|---|
| 14 Å tunneling limit | The farthest an electron can tunnel between proteins (1.4 billionths of a meter), which he credits to Dutton |
| 160 vs 78 | His numbers for structured, charge-holding cell water (160) versus ordinary bulk water (78) |
| 30 million volts per meter | The electric field he says the thin inner mitochondrial membrane holds, likened to lightning |
| 71-terahertz signal | A vibration found in mitochondria (Song et al., 2026) that he reads as a timing signal pacing the nucleus and telomeres |
| Agonic line | A line where compass north equals true north |
| Angular momentum | The quantity of spin or rotation, which cannot simply appear or vanish |
| Atavistic retreat | Tissues reverting to older, simpler, looser designs after the chromosome 2 fuse blows |
| ATP / ATP synthase | The cell's energy currency / the rotary motor in mitochondria that makes it |
| Bandgap | The energy step that lets a protein act as a semiconductor; he says it narrows when water collapses |
| Base chain / layer two | His Bitcoin analogy: DNA is the base chain; daily clock-timed tagging is the "layer two" that adjusts genes without rewriting them |
| Base-chain "atomic mass" | Methyl and acetyl tags and deuterium adding physical weight to DNA and slowing it |
| Biological LENR | Low-energy nuclear reactions he proposes happen at crista tips, such as copper-63 becoming copper-65 |
| Biological Nazaré | Crista tips focusing a proton wave like the Nazaré canyon focuses ocean swells |
| Biological SCRAM | A cell's emergency shutdown (term borrowed from nuclear reactors) when field loss breaches the membrane |
| Bus bar | His term for the junctions linking mitochondria into one shared power network |
| Cardiolipin | A cone-shaped fat in the inner membrane that he calls its insulating sleeve |
| cGAS-STING | A cell alarm triggered by stray DNA that drives inflammation |
| Cell danger response | Robert Naviaux's term for a cell's defensive shutdown mode |
| Chiral / chirality | Having a "handedness," like left and right hands |
| Chromosome 2 fuse | The leftover telomere loop where two ape chromosomes fused, which he says "blows" under extreme stress |
| CISS | Chiral-induced spin selectivity: one-handed molecules letting through electrons of one spin only |
| Clock dampener | His term for FOXO3 pausing cell division to slow the cell clock and allow repair |
| CO₂–bicarbonate loop | Using carbonated water and retained carbon dioxide to build bicarbonate that he says draws deuterium out |
| Corrupt bit | A misfolded protein whose information has turned to noise |
| Cristae | The inward folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane |
| Cyanide-like lock | A whole-chain electron stall caused physically by field loss rather than by poison |
| Cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) | The last enzyme of the electron chain, where electrons meet oxygen |
| De-frag | Shaking deuterium loose and restoring order in tissue |
| Deuterium | Heavy hydrogen, with an extra neutron, about twice as heavy |
| Deuterium-depleted water (DDW) | Water low in heavy hydrogen; he says healthy mitochondria make it |
| Diamagnetic | Weakly pushed away by a magnet; his description of singlet oxygen |
| Diamond vs coal | His image for ordered 160 water versus disordered 78 water, drawn from entropy-based gravity papers |
| Dielectric constant | How much electric charge a substance can hold and how strongly it buffers electric fields |
| Doping / rogue dopant | Controlled impurities that tune a semiconductor / foreign chemicals that trap electrons |
| DRT3 / DRT7 | Template-free DNA-writing systems from bacteria that he says rebuild telomere caps |
| Electromagnetic terroir | Gene activity following the local magnetic field, as wine follows its soil |
| Energy resistance (Picard) | Martin Picard's idea of how much the cell resists energy flow; health is a "just-right" zone |
| Entropy | Accumulated disorder |
| Epigenetic drift | Slow change in the chemical marks on DNA, which he treats as a passenger of field damage |
| Exclusion-zone (EZ) water | Gerald Pollack's ordered water layer that forms against surfaces; his 160 state |
| Fetal / adult matrix freeze | Tethered cord / adhesive arachnoiditis, seen as one spinal lattice lock at different ages |
| Ferron | An iron core in ferritin or cytochromes acting as a tiny magnetic compass and capacitor |
| FOXO3 | A gene common in very long-lived people that can pause cell division |
| Fourth ventricle | A fluid space in the brainstem where he says the body filters deuterium |
| Geodynamo | The churning of Earth's core that generates its magnetic field |
| Glycolysis | Breaking down sugar without mitochondria (fermentation) |
| Grounding | Direct skin contact with the earth, or a low-resistance indoor connection to it |
| Heteroplasmy | The share of damaged mitochondrial DNA in a cell |
| Horvath clock | A test estimating "biological age" from DNA marks, which he says not to rely on |
| HRV | Heart-rate variability: beat-to-beat variation in heart timing, his readout of lattice lock |
| Hydroxyproline | A key collagen building block whose formation he says needs triplet oxygen |
| Hyper-local magnetic excursion | Wireless devices disrupting the field around a person like a small field collapse |
| Isotope shield | Structured water keeping deuterium away from the nucleus and DNA |
| Landauer erasure | Clearing corrupted information (such as a misfolded protein), which must release heat |
| Landauer liquidation | Stored order lost as heat on a large scale, draining ATP and NAD⁺ |
| Landauer's principle | Rolf Landauer's 1961 finding that erasing information releases a minimum amount of heat |
| Larmor radius | The radius of the circle an electron traces in a magnetic field; he says it widens as the field weakens |
| Lattice lock | Stiff, deuterium-loaded body water with a jammed energy motor and a cell blind to sunlight |
| Magnetic sovereignty | Taking control of your own magnetic environment |
| Marcus inversion (inverted region) | Electron transfer slowing once the driving force passes its optimum, so pushing harder moves fewer electrons |
| Marcus inversion well | A place or season that holds a person in that stalled state |
| Mitochondria | The tiny power units inside cells |
| NAD⁺ | The cell's main electron-carrier molecule |
| NADD+ | His term for deuterium-loaded NAD⁺ |
| Near-infrared deficiency | Lacking usable red and infrared light, even in full sun when unpinned |
| Neuropsin (OPN5) | A sensor for dawn UV-A light in eye and skin |
| nnEMF | Non-native electromagnetic fields: man-made wireless and electrical fields |
| Non-dipole field | The irregular, three-dimensional parts of Earth's field, including reversed-polarity patches |
| Optico-Magnetic Engineering | His name for the shared physics he sees in the Sun and the mitochondrion |
| Ortho- / para-water | Water whose hydrogen nuclei spin in step ("grease," his 78 state) / whose spins cancel ("superconductor," his 160 state) |
| p53 | The protein that guards the genome, triggering repair, cell retirement or death |
| Paramagnetic | Weakly drawn to a magnet; his description of triplet oxygen and wet DNA |
| Paramagnetic gate | Complex IV's field-backed checkpoint matching electrons to oxygen's spin |
| Personal Quantum Solvency | Accepting that food and drugs can't fix a field problem, and acting on location, light and grounding |
| Phase separation | Proteins dropping out of solution into separate clumps, which he links to spinal scarring |
| Photonic purge | Morning near-infrared freeing nitric oxide stuck on cytochrome c oxidase |
| Pinned / unpinned | Living under a stable, mostly horizontal local field while grounded and sunlit, or not |
| Polymorphous light eruption | A spring sun rash, which he calls sun allergy and reads as a deuterium flush |
| Primary water | Water from deep volcanic fissures, which he says helps rebuild structured water |
| Proteasome | The cell's protein shredder |
| Pseudohypoxia | A cell acting oxygen-starved even though oxygen is present |
| Quantum blockchain | His name for the four genes clustered around the chromosome 2 fusion site |
| Quantum synchrotron | His image of the mitochondrion (and the Sun) as a low-energy particle accelerator |
| Radiant smoke | Chaotic light leaking from a locked cell |
| Radical pair | Two molecules each holding one unpaired electron, whose spin balance a weak field can tip |
| Respiratory (electron) chain | The series of protein complexes that pass electrons from food to oxygen |
| Safe mode | The body's shutdown into primitive designs after the chromosome 2 fuse blows, likened to satellites in the South Atlantic Anomaly |
| Senescence | A cell's permanent retirement from dividing |
| Singlet oxygen | Oxygen with paired spins: reactive and damaging in his model |
| South Atlantic Anomaly | A large weak patch in Earth's magnetic field |
| Spaceflight sickness without the rocket | Chronic illness caused by indoor, ungrounded, wireless-soaked living that mimics orbit |
| Sphenoid Venturi | His drain point behind the nose where heavy water should be filtered |
| Spin barrier | Triplet oxygen's inability to react directly with ordinary molecules, which he says makes aerobic life possible |
| Structural time permanence | His term for a slowed, protected cell state that preserves genetic information |
| Superoxide pulses | Brief oxygen-radical signals he calls healthy Morse code, which he says diabetics have exhausted |
| Telomeres | Protective caps on the ends of chromosomes, which he says record entropy rather than age |
| Thermal hot potato | His image for singlet oxygen doing no work and only producing damaging heat |
| Three turns sense, seven turns resonate | His rule that a seven-turn spiral pushes water toward the structured state |
| Time-stamping | The daily clock deciding when proteins receive their chemical tags |
| Topological insurance | Marcus inversion acting as a built-in circuit breaker against thermal meltdown |
| TRCS | Telomere/rDNA Co-Regulation model: shortening of telomeres and DNA repeats drives aging through p53 |
| Triplet oxygen | Ordinary oxygen with parallel spins: the safe form in his model |
| Tunnel diode | An electronic part in which raising voltage lowers current, his analogy for Marcus inversion |
| Tunneling | A particle slipping through an energy barrier it could not climb over |
| Ubiquitin | A small protein "delete" label that marks other proteins for shredding |
| Ubiquitin–G3P pivot | His local repair step: ubiquitin brings glucose so a misfolded protein can refold before being erased |
| Unfolded-protein response | The alarm raised when proteins fold badly; his cell "error log" |
| UPE | Ultra-weak photon emission: faint light given off by cells |
| Volcano Protocol | His routine of carbonated water with DDW, a back water jet with a 40 Hz fork, and sunrise grounding in a pinned place |
| Warburg shift | Cells falling back on fermentation instead of mitochondrial burning, which he treats as a survival mode |
| Water battery / water table | His terms for the cell's charge-holding structured water |
| Worse antenna | Nerve, pigment and skin tissue shrinking so the body picks up less electromagnetic noise |
| Zeeman handshake | Earth's field keeping the CISS spin filter aligned |
| Zone 2 | Steady moderate-intensity endurance exercise |
