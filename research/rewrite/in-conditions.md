### Cancer as a voltage failure
- **id:** cancer-voltage
- **pillar:** support
- **summary:** Kruse says any lasting fall in the voltage of the mitochondria's inner membrane can produce cancer, a protective retreat that the environment, not drugs, must reverse.
- **protocols:** cancer-glioma

Kruse sees cancer as an electrical failure rather than mainly a genetic one. In his model the cell's power plants, the mitochondria, hold a strong charge across their inner membrane, and anything that lets that voltage sag for long enough can tip a cell into cancer; he even names the fields around wind turbines, given years of exposure. The tumor, to him, is a retreat the cell makes to survive, so the fix lies in restoring the environment rather than in drugs.

#### How a cell falls into cancer

Kruse describes a healthy cell at about −70 millivolts and a failing one at −15 to −30. At that lower level, he says, a gate in the inner membrane (UCP-2) loses the voltage that keeps it sealed. Water rich in deuterium, a heavy form of hydrogen with an extra neutron, then floods the mitochondria's interior.

In his account deuterium's extra mass brakes ATP synthase, the spinning motor that makes the cell's energy currency, ATP. Complex IV, the last step of the energy chain, then stops making the cell's own clean, low-deuterium water. Without that water the field across the inner membrane short-circuits.

The cell then falls back on fermentation, the ancient oxygen-free way of burning sugar, and begins dividing out of control. This switch is called the Warburg shift, after Otto Warburg, who saw cancer as damaged respiration rather than mutation; Kruse says his voltage model completes Warburg's picture.

#### The "matrix fire" and its causes

Kruse calls the stalled interior a "matrix fire." Oxygen that can't be used cleanly flips into singlet oxygen, a reactive form that attacks membranes and DNA, and the faint light cells naturally give off (ultra-weak photon emission) turns into chaotic noise. The growth pathways cancer drugs target (PI3K, Akt, mTOR) are, in his reading, readouts of that fire, not its cause.

He names four things that uncouple Complex IV: missing near-infrared light; nnEMF (non-native electromagnetic fields from wireless devices and wiring); deuterium; and the wrong copper isotope, since he says Complex IV is built to run on heavy copper-65. Like weight gain, he suggests, cancer can be a "safe mode": a lower-energy state that staves off sudden organ failure and buys time, which can be won back once the interference is removed.

#### Magnetism, the brain and the night

Magnetism sits at the root of his model. Kruse says oxygen keeps its safe, stable form (triplet oxygen) only when a person is "pinned": grounded, sunlit and living under a stable local magnetic field. His shortest chain for glioma, a brain tumor, is no pinning, then singlet oxygen, then glioma. Glioblastoma, he argues, starts when foreign magnetic fields break the junctions that link mitochondria into one network, and restoring that connection can extend survival beyond what doctors expect.

Because Earth's field is weakening, he expects a magnetic excursion (a large, rapid drop and shift in the field) to drive many cancers. Without spin pinning, chromosomes misalign as cells divide, and the mutations oncology tracks are debris left by that failure.

Night is part of the picture. Citing studies from Russel Reiter's group, Kruse says cancer cells drift back toward normal oxygen-burning at night, when mitochondria release melatonin, and return to fermentation by day. He calls this nightly repair "magnetic chemotherapy," and says light at night switches it off.

#### In practice

His priorities for cancer and glioma: get magnetically pinned first; ground; take natural UV-A and infrared sunlight and don't fear UV-B; remove nnEMF; and for glioma, sleep in a cold bedroom. At high latitudes, he adds, naturally low-deuterium water won't protect someone who lives indoors under artificial light. He calls a ketogenic diet unwise as a cancer strategy because many aggressive tumors (prostate, glioblastoma, triple-negative breast) burn fat too, so cutting glucose alone can select fat-burning clones. Nor does he see drugs as the main answer, pointing to a 15-year Oxford trial in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in which surgery or radiation for localized prostate cancer did not lengthen survival compared with monitoring.

### Diabetes and blood sugar

Kruse treats diabetes as a problem of light, deuterium and magnetism, not of food. In type 2, Kruse says, the beta cell (the pancreas cell that makes insulin) is usually full of insulin but can't release it. The vagus nerve drives an enzyme, carbonic anhydrase, to make bicarbonate, which binds deuterium and carries it out through the pancreatic ducts into the gut. Sugar under blue light, or a magnetic excursion, knocks the enzyme out of alignment and "welds the tailpipe shut," so deuterium backs up into the beta cells.

The morning rise in blood sugar, the "dawn phenomenon," is in his reading a failure to catch the morning magnetic shift, not a hormone surge. A diabetic's damaged membranes can't capture it, so voltage drops and the liver dumps glucose to keep the cell's battery alive.

Kruse calls diabetics "magnetic messes" who all need an agonic line, a place where compass north and true north coincide, which he treats as the zone of most coherent field. #### Type 1

Type 1 he frames as a deuterium and magnetic problem too. Blue light without sunlight's near-infrared can't be used, so the body stores the energy as deuterium in tissue and fat ("mammals who turn blue light into fat"), and the pancreas, a midline isotope regulator, stops filtering. He accepts that light and field alone may not regrow beta cells the immune system has destroyed. For those cases he points to stem-cell-derived beta cells and plasmapheresis (filtering antibodies out of the blood plasma), each protected by sun and pinning.

- **pillar:** support
- **summary:** Kruse calls cholesterol the membrane's insulator and light sensor, and traces heart and artery disease to lost melanin, lost field and missing sun.

Kruse rejects the idea that cholesterol causes heart disease. #### Cholesterol as insulator and light sensor

Kruse calls cholesterol the main stabilizer of the inner mitochondrial membrane: it sets how stiff and well insulated the membrane is, which lets it hold its strong electric field. Heart attacks were barely recognized before about 1911, Kruse notes, and he ties the rise of heart disease to Earth's weakening field since the 1859 Carrington Event and to electrification. Charged by light, melanin holds metals and calcium in solution, and light-activated vitamin K2 keeps calcium bound to its proteins. When nnEMF (non-native electromagnetic fields) and blue light make melanin drop its metals, calcium is left in the artery wall like a scab, so calcified arteries and valves need the field restored, not calcium-channel blockers or peptides.

High blood pressure is, to Kruse, the loss of a "second pump": blood moving through Earth's field is pushed along by a magnetic (Lorentz) force, and when that assist fades the heart has to push harder. Light at night, he adds, remodels the heart.

#### One root with the brain

Heart and brain disease share one root in his view: failure of the body's non-visual light sensing, the light sensors in skin and tissue outside the eye's vision. Strong sun on the chest lets skin melanin release adenosine, the same molecule emergency doctors use to reset a racing heart, which steadies rhythm. He says sunlight lowers Lp(a), ApoE and calcium scores. Atrial fibrillation set off through the vagus nerve he traces to a deuterium-loaded cardiac nerve plexus.

ALS he calls a waking form of dream-sleep paralysis. With the body clock destroyed, the brainstem can't tell day fields from night fields and sends the paralysis commands of REM sleep while the person is awake. He says it is rare in tanned tropical people and worsened by long flights across time zones, especially over the weakening South Atlantic field.

MS, in his account, is spin failure at the nodes of Ranvier, the gaps in a nerve's insulation where signals jump. Its rise with latitude reflects collapsing water structure and melanin, not low vitamin D, and the misfolded proteins in MS are readouts of a poor local field.
