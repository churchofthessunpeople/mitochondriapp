| Term | Plain meaning (in his usage) |
|---|---|
| Stator | The planet's spinning mass (true north) treated as the fixed reference part of a motor, against which the wandering dynamo is measured |
| Error rate (declination) | Declination read as the mismatch between Earth's spin and its magnetic dynamo; the wider it is, the worse body rhythms and spins mesh |
| Input / exhaust | Field lines gathering toward the north ("input") and spreading apart over the south ("exhaust"), which he says leaves the Southern Hemisphere more exposed |
| Magnetic dam | A strong horizontal field component that holds protons in place; he says it once made parts of the Southern Hemisphere livable |
| Magnetic fog | Local iron, basalt and wireless fields at the surface that mask the core's signal in ground readings |
| Hyperfine noise trap | Extra nuclear-spin "noise" (for example from deuterium) that makes a radical-pair magnetic sensor less sensitive to weak fields |
| Local stator rule | His rule that the rock underfoot, not global averages, decides the field a body actually experiences |
| Brunhes–Matuyama reversal | The last full swap of Earth's magnetic poles, about 780,000 years ago; older rock set before it can oppose today's field |
| Current of injury | Robert Becker's term for the small natural electric current that steers tissue repair |
| Maillard oven | A body or place that "browns" tissue from inside, like food browning when cooked, through heat, heavy hydrogen and lost grounding |
| Mammalian sphinxing | Facing east at sunrise on all fours so hands and feet touch the earth while catching dawn light |
| Dielectric constant (160 vs 78) | How well water stores charge; he scores structured cell water at about 160 and ordinary or disordered water at 78 |
| mTOR | The cell's main growth signal, which he says quiets when grounding and sunlight supply electrons |
| Cap cloud ("capilla") | A cloud that sits on a volcano's summit; to him, water held in place by the rock's field and a sign of a healthy spot |
| Magnetic stall | A local failure of the field, signalled for example by a humid-region volcano losing its cloud cap |
| Motional induction | The small field produced when salty, conductive waves move over magnetic black sand |
| Mechanical dither | Regular earthquake shaking that, in his account, clears background noise so clock genes can pick up Earth's rhythm |
| Reverse volcano | His name for a pyramid built to copy a volcano's magnetic and water-ordering effect away from volcanic ground |
| Inductance–capacitance (LC) circuit | The simplest tuned antenna; how he explains an open copper ring gathering and concentrating natural frequencies |
| Mayan trap | Choosing a place for one factor alone (such as an agonic line) while neglecting light and water |
| Dynamo-Isotopic Calculator | The tool he says should replace the UV index by weighing sun, field and local deuterium together |
| Induction zone | An active fault zone (such as New Madrid) that gives off electromagnetic signals under stress and makes the local field spiky |
| Isotopic gearbox | A mitochondrial lineage seen as a fixed gear ratio for ATP synthase, matched to the field and sun where it arose |
| Human Lagrangian | His proposed equation linking field strength, melanin and solar dose to the ratio of light to heavy hydrogen a cell should hold at a given place |
| Singlet flashover | Overheating of a northern ("leaky") engine under strong UV or radiation, which he links to melanoma and rapid aging |
| Isotopic insufflation | Deuterium and tritium drawn down from the upper atmosphere into water and food where the field is weak |
| Janitor (field as) | His image of a healthy field sweeping deuterium to the edges of the body's flows so it can be cleared |
| Siberian jerk | The fast run of the north magnetic pole toward Siberia, which he says strains the southern weak spot |
| Ground fault (SAA) | His description of the South Atlantic Anomaly as a break in the ocean's electrical circuit that stalls the Atlantic current |
| Isotopic hangover | The fall in melatonin 15–33 hours after a geomagnetic spike |
| Magnetic flatline | The very weak field of the Ediacaran period just before the Cambrian |
| Ohmic heating | Heat released by electrical resistance; here, heat from a failing dynamo's currents |
| Chandler wobble | A small natural wobble of Earth's spin axis whose near-disappearance he reads as the planet's "transmission in neutral" |
| Lorentz force | The push a magnetic field gives to moving charged particles, such as those in salty seawater |
| Magnetic Arctic flashover | His term for Arctic warming and northward-spreading insects read as a magnetic, not greenhouse, failure |
| Quantum blockers | Hospital monitors and lights that, he says, keep a stalled body from restarting |
| Piezoelectric | Able to turn pressure (and, in his usage, fields) into tiny currents, as he says insect chitin does |
| Optically pumped magnetometer | A sensitive laser-based field sensor he names as the better ground instrument |
