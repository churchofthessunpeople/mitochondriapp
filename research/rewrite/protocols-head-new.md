## Light

### Sunrise light on bare eyes and skin
- **id:** sunrise-horizon
- **pillar:** light
- **app:** ready
- **summary:** Be outside every morning as the sun rises, with its first light on bare eyes and skin.
- **steps:**
  1. Get up before sunrise so you are outside when the sun clears the horizon.
  2. Take off sunglasses, contact lenses and glasses, and don't watch from behind window glass.
  3. Face east toward the rising sun, without staring straight into it.
  4. Uncover as much skin as you reasonably can.
  5. Stand barefoot at the same time — surf, wet sand, dewy grass or basalt (see barefoot-earth). Variations: face east on all fours while grounded ("mammalian sphinxing"), or lie with your spine on the ground.
  6. Stay 30–45 minutes, within the first 60–90 minutes after sunrise; longer is fine, up to about three hours on a beach.
  7. Repeat every morning.
  8. Watch for a well-formed (Bristol type 4) stool as a sign the routine is working.
  9. For brain fog, pair this routine with dropping chemical fixes and switching routers off before sleep.
- **equipment:** none
- **why:** sunrise-why
- **sources:** [382093](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382093/), [382666](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382666/), [383983](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/383983/), [377098](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/377098/), [374488](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/374488/)

### Midday sun and UV-B, unfiltered
- **id:** sun-exposure
- **pillar:** light
- **app:** ready
- **summary:** Get regular midday sun, UV-B included, on bare skin with no sunscreen or sunglasses, stopping before any redness.
- **steps:**
  1. Go out near solar noon, when your shadow is shorter than you are.
  2. Expose torso and back; tan with bare skin, not through clothing.
  3. Leave off sunscreen, sunglasses, contacts and glasses outdoors.
  4. Stand barefoot on the ground while you do it.
  5. Build your time up gradually; move into shade or put on light clothing at the first warmth.
  6. Prioritize midday UV-B around the winter solstice, and if you are not yet "pinned" (grounded, sunlit and living under a stable local magnetic field).
  7. For dry skin, use morning sun, cold exposure and grounding rather than lotion or extra drinking water.
  8. After a shower, use at most tallow or coconut oil — no petroleum- or silicone-based lotions, and no moisturizers with hidden UV filters.
  9. Go out on cloudy days too.
  10. Northern maternal lineages (such as haplogroup H) living in the tropics: avoid long exposure between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m. unless you are moving or grounded.
- **equipment:** none
- **cautions:** Never burn on purpose; if you redden, cut back. Don't use sunscreen or sunglasses. Leave UV-C lamps alone.
- **why:** solar-noon-vitamin-d, melanin-semiconductor
- **sources:** [382093](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382093/), [383426](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/383426/), [378979](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/378979/), [377533](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/377533/), [376133](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/376133/)

### Four-phase sun day (building a "solar callus")
- **id:** four-phase-sun-day
- **pillar:** light
- **app:** ready
- **summary:** A timed daily light schedule — sunrise, mid-morning, midday and sunset — for adapting skin to strong or tropical sun without burning.
- **steps:**
  1. Sunrise phase: within 60–90 minutes after the sun clears the horizon, spend 30–45 minutes in its deep red and near-infrared light (no UV yet) with bare eyes and as much bare skin as possible, feet on volcanic basalt or wet black sand.
  2. Mid-morning UV-A phase (about 8:30–11:00, depending on season and place): 15–25 minutes with skin and eyes open to the sky while walking or moving; never sunglasses, contacts or glasses.
  3. Midday UV-B phase (shadow shorter than your height, about 11:30–1:30): 3–10 minutes at most on torso and back while grounded on bare rock.
  4. If you're not yet adapted, start the midday phase at 3 minutes and add 1 minute every few days as your tan deepens.
  5. At the first slight sting or warmth, move into shade or put on long, light clothing.
  6. Sunset phase: in the last hour of daylight, 20–30 minutes of skin exposure to red and infrared (no UV) while winding down.
  7. Even once adapted, avoid artificial light at night and out-of-season food eaten under the noon sun.
  8. Optional: saffron, to speed adaptation.
- **equipment:** none (basalt or black sand ideal)
- **cautions:** Never let skin redden. Northern lineages moving into tropical sun should acclimate gradually.
- **why:** solar-callus, haplogroup-zipcode
- **sources:** [382093](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382093/), [382091](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382091/), [382089](https://forum.jackkruse.com/posts/382089/)

### Evening light: incandescent, red, dark
- **id:** blue-light-hygiene
- **pillar:** light
- **app:** ready
- **summary:** After sunset, use only warm, blue-free light; in daylight, never block blue.
- **steps:**
  1. Replace LED and fluorescent bulbs with incandescent ones.
  2. In daylight, wear no blue blockers of any tint, red or yellow included.
  3. By day, open a window rather than buying lamps that imitate the sun.

  5. Don't eat sugary food under screens or LEDs at night.
  6. Never wear sunglasses. If you wear contact lenses, go back to glasses, and take the glasses off whenever you're outdoors.
  7. Sleep in total darkness, with no artificial light at night.
- **equipment:** incandescent and red bulbs; 100% blue-blocking glasses

### Drinking water: reverse osmosis plus UV
- **id:** drinking-water
- **pillar:** water
- **app:** ready
- **summary:** Drink reverse-osmosis water with a UV stage, or clean deep-source water; avoid hard, mineral-heavy, fluoridated or surface water.
- **steps:**
  1. Filter drinking water by reverse osmosis followed by a UV stage.
  2. Avoid fluoride.
  3. Avoid municipal water lines in heavily wired urban areas, and surface-runoff river water.
  4. Don't rehydrate on hard, metal-laden well water.
  5. Prefer deep "primary water" (from fissures in volcanic rock) or ancient artesian wells to surface water; clean local spring water drunk in the sun is a good natural mineral source. Judge a bottled spring water (such as Mountain Valley) by the hydrology of its source; desert sources count against it.
  6. Never drink from the Riviera Maya cenote system. On Central America's Pacific side, make a new water plan, since local rain is getting heavier in deuterium.
  7. Prefer copper plumbing to plastic.
  8. Store drinking water in large copper vessels and let it sit 4–16 hours before drinking.
  9. If local animals show signs of decline, switch to deep aquifer water (see track-your-field).
- **equipment:** RO filter with UV stage; optional copper storage vessels
- **why:** deuterium-and-water
